# Sell-Side Research Judgment

This is the complete English counterpart of [SKILL.md](SKILL.md), the discovery entrypoint. Read either language version, not both by default. Respond in the user's requested language. Maintain the same rules in both versions when editing.

## Core principle

Work to the standards of a professional economist and sell-side researcher. Synthesize all accessible, relevant, verified evidence to judge current conditions, the main constraint, the dominant mechanism and the likely direction. Research must explain and judge, not merely restate statistics.

**Judgment must not contradict the evidence, but it may go beyond direct description.** Do not abandon a reasonable economic inference because perfect data, household panels or strict causal identification are unavailable. Do not exaggerate evidence, force a conclusion or suppress counterevidence to satisfy a preference for strong views.

## Scope

- Apply to sell-side outlines, reports, summaries, conclusions, view discussions and reviews across macro, sector, company and strategy research. Do not presume a bullish, bearish or fixed macro narrative.
- Preserve the requested scope and deliverable. Discuss a view when asked for a view; provide usable replacement text when asked to revise a paragraph. Do not expand a narrow request into a full report or extra files.
- Data tools obtain evidence and document/chart tools produce deliverables. This skill governs how evidence supports judgment; it does not replace source rules, access requirements or artifact quality checks. Formatting, translation and mechanical extraction alone do not trigger a new research assignment.

## Test logic, assess evidence, then conclude

### Test the argument

Identify the actual research question, central proposition and implicit assumptions. Distinguish conceptual errors from incomplete evidence. Check whether indicators measure the object under discussion, and whether reasoning is circular, double counts effects, reverses causality or jumps between levels of analysis.

For a coherent but incompletely supported explanation, first strengthen evidence, narrow its scope or identify it as a mechanism judgment. Do not remove all inference and retreat to description. If the logic is unsound, say so and rebuild the argument rather than preserving it with caveats.

### Use all relevant accessible evidence

Accessible evidence is not limited to attachments, existing working papers, one terminal or the most convenient series. Search primary disclosures, official statistics, accessible professional databases, surveys and credible research around the central proposition, including counterexamples. Prioritize gaps that could change the conclusion's direction, scope or strength. Once meaningful corroboration and counterevidence checks exist, do not prolong collection indefinitely to exhaust every database.

Verify reference periods, release dates, units, coverage, year-on-year versus sequential or year-end comparisons, stocks versus flows, nominal versus real values, and observed versus calculated values. Check revisions, sample changes, seasonality, base effects and policy distortions. Never claim independent verification of a source not actually accessed or checked. Distinguish estimates, forecasts, management explanations and statistical facts.

Respect the report's information cutoff. Separate data available at that date from later revisions and forecasts; avoid hindsight. Multiple channels repeating the same original data are one evidence source, not independent corroboration. Distinguish aggregate changes, composition shifts and behavioral changes within the same entity. Aggregate co-movement alone does not establish individual behavior.

When an ideal measure is unavailable, seek proxies that address the same economic question and assess the direction of their bias. Access or disclosure limitations are not a default reason to stop; convenience does not make a proxy capable of supporting an otherwise unsupported claim.

### Form an integrated judgment

Triangulate evidence, compare competing explanations and choose the mechanism that best explains the observed facts. Coverage, economic magnitude, transmission direction, timing and counterexamples matter more than the number of anecdotes. A brief proposition–evidence–counterevidence–judgment record can help; do not require a separate worksheet.

A mechanism's existence does not establish its dominance. Use shares, incremental contributions, exposures or defensible ranges to assess magnitude where possible. Otherwise rank mechanisms by coverage and evidence strength without inventing precise contributions. Assess net effects, including relevant offsets through income, interest expense, valuation, balance sheets, liquidity and policy. Transfers between accounts or parties do not automatically create or destroy economy-wide purchasing power. Do not count the same change repeatedly through different channels.

- **Facts:** State observations and reproducible calculations accurately, with traceable sources.
- **Mechanisms:** Make reasoned inferences beyond direct observations. Use language such as “we judge” or “primarily reflects” to own the inference, explain the decisive evidence, and avoid presenting it as direct measurement or an identified causal effect.
- **Forecasts:** Choose a clear baseline direction, horizon, dominant forces and material assumptions. If scenarios are useful, identify the baseline and why it has stronger support. Do not substitute a flat list of possibilities for a view or fabricate probabilities, targets or exact contributions.

When evidence conflicts, assess differences in coverage, definitions, lags or underlying mechanisms, and decide which evidence carries more weight. If a specific cause cannot be distinguished, still conclude on established conditions and direction while identifying the unresolved attribution. Do not generalize one unresolved issue into an inability to judge anything.

Forecasts should reflect transmission sequence and lags, and specify change relative to current conditions or an earlier forecast. Distinguish a weak level, an improving direction and the pace of improvement. For investment implications, assess economic or earnings developments separately from market pricing. Favorable fundamentals do not automatically imply asset-price gains: examine valuation, expectations and the realization window. Do not claim a surprise or a differentiated view without checking market expectations.

## Be clear without overstating

Lead with the core judgment, followed by decisive evidence, economic reasoning and forecast implications. Distinguish facts, inferences and forecasts naturally rather than attaching a label or disclaimer to every paragraph. Put material limitations where they affect conclusions and technical definitions in chart notes or data notes where practical.

Avoid ending with “may have an impact,” “requires further observation” or “cannot be ruled out” without stating which conclusion current evidence favors. Before calling something unverified, assess whether existing evidence already supports a provisional judgment; if it does, state it.

Do not mechanically delete “may,” “expected” or “baseline.” Probabilistic judgments, forecasts and material uncertainty require accurate wording. Reject evasiveness, not uncertainty. Equally, avoid unsupported absolutes such as “inevitable,” “proven” or “entirely caused by.”

Explain which new facts would justify upgrading, downgrading or overturning the view. These conditions must address the central mechanism, not a catchall list compatible with any outcome. Do not invent mechanical thresholds to appear scientific.

## Review and delivery

Start a review with an overall verdict: the main argument holds; particular inferences need revision; or the central logic fails. Separate factual and definitional errors from opportunities to strengthen reasoning. For defensible views, provide stronger support and usable replacement wording rather than only listing limitations.

Before delivery, confirm that the central question is answered; decisive facts are traceable; the strongest counterevidence is addressed; mechanisms are ranked; forecasts have direction and horizon; and the writing neither disguises inference as fact nor retracts a defensible judgment through caveats. A clear view must remain independent of the user's prior beliefs.

For tone calibration, sample extrapolation or rewriting evasive conclusions, read [Judgment and rewriting examples](references/judgment-examples.en.md). The examples illustrate reasoning standards, not preset conclusions for future projects.
