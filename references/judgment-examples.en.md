# Judgment and rewriting examples

These are methodological examples, not real data for citation or preset views on a market or economic cycle. They correspond to the [Chinese examples](judgment-examples.md).

“Appropriate” passages demonstrate report-ready economic judgments. The verification notes that follow guide research and review; do not copy every note into the body. Retain information that materially changes the conclusion and consolidate technical definitions in chart or data notes.

## 1. Judge the dominant mechanism without claiming identified causality

Given: several measures show rising financial assets, declining asset income and consumption growing more slowly than income; housing prices remain under pressure. No panel links the same households' assets and spending.

Evasive: “Financial-asset growth may affect consumption, but further study is needed without microdata.”

Overstated: “Bank data prove that deposit-rate cuts caused national consumption to decline.”

Appropriate: “Financial-asset growth currently provides weak support for consumption. Lower asset income, housing-wealth pressure and a declining propensity to consume jointly suggest that households are prioritizing balance-sheet repair; expanding assets have not translated into a corresponding increase in spending budgets.”

This makes a directional and mechanism judgment without inventing a consumption contribution. If consumption is growing, do not describe an absolute decline. Include strong evidence of debt-service relief or other property income when judging the net direction rather than ignoring it to protect the thesis.

## 2. Use a sample for structural inference, not a fabricated national measurement

Given: at one bank, high-asset clients account for a rising share of retail assets under management (AUM) and a substantial share of the increase. Other banks report growth in higher client tiers without comparable AUM figures.

Evasive: “Bank samples are not nationally representative, so wealth distribution cannot be discussed.”

Overstated: “A precise percentage of all new national wealth belongs to high-net-worth households.”

Appropriate: “The distribution of asset growth constrains its transmission to broad household consumption. Banks' managed-asset expansion is concentrated among higher-asset clients. Together with research on wealth distribution and consumption behavior, the distribution of gains and differences in spending propensity point to a weaker consumption response.”

Precise sample ratios apply only to that sample. Threshold migration, client transfers and valuation affect the attribution of growth. The relationship between assets and marginal propensity to consume needs separate support; bank AUM does not directly measure spending propensity. Search for that support before using the mechanism, or retain only the established distributional finding.

## 3. Retain investment enthusiasm as an explanation while correcting flow narratives

Given: wider market participation and active margin trading coincide with a lower propensity to consume. Household surveys show stronger investment intentions but weak spending plans, while income and housing constraints remain substantial. A subsequent market correction occurs while net flows differ across ETF categories.

Evasive: “Without tracing money into consumption accounts, stock-market evidence must be excluded.”

Overstated: “Equity-ETF outflows mean households shifted into bonds and never spent the money.”

Appropriate: “Investment enthusiasm is primarily lifting allocation demand, with limited support for broad consumption. Stronger household investment intentions alongside weak spending plans indicate that return-seeking exceeds the expansion of current spending. Positive equity wealth effects have yet to overcome income and housing constraints; active capital markets and a slow consumption recovery are set to coexist in the near term.”

Institution-heavy product totals cannot directly represent households. Pre-event spending cannot prove a post-event effect. Weak aggregate spending alone does not establish a negative equity wealth effect; assess its importance relative to income, housing and other forces.

## 4. Choose a forecast baseline that can be overturned

Evasive: “Demand could strengthen or weaken; policy and further data need watching.”

Appropriate: “Demand is expected to grow slowly over the next two quarters. Income growth provides support, but consumption propensity and housing constraints limit acceleration. Services should outperform, while demand's transmission to final prices remains slow.”

This is a structural illustration. Actual direction, horizon and leading variables must follow project-specific evidence. Do not add unsupported probabilities or targets, or retain a baseline once contrary evidence dominates.

Retain upgrade conditions in research notes, such as a persistent rise in consumption propensity and broader housing stabilization. Discuss them together in the outlook or risks when useful; do not append a conditional sentence to every body paragraph.

## 5. Higher prices do not automatically imply stronger demand

Given: sector prices rise while orders and sales volumes remain weak, alongside production cuts and higher input costs.

Evasive: “Price increases are complex and may reflect both supply and demand.”

Appropriate: “This price recovery is driven primarily by supply contraction and costs; final demand has yet to take over. Persistently weak orders and volumes constrain downstream pricing, so the near-term recovery will remain concentrated in segments with pronounced supply cuts or cost increases.”

Explain why supply and cost evidence outweighs alternatives. If new orders and real volumes strengthen broadly, revise the view rather than treating weak demand as a fixed template.

## 6. Research judgment does not excuse technical errors

Year-to-date versus year-on-year comparisons, gross sales versus net inflows, average versus closing balances, growth indices versus price levels, and revised versus unrevised values can materially change conclusions. Correct calculations or charts first, then reassess the thesis.

Appropriate review: “The main argument holds. The chart's mixed comparison periods must be corrected. After correction, both indicators still support strong allocation demand but do not quantify deposit migration. Retain the directional view and replace the monetary attribution.”

Do not equate factual correction with a lack of conviction, or use technical limitations to discard a still-supported economic conclusion.

## 7. Upgrade when the evidence improves

Given: orders, sales and capacity utilization improve repeatedly; inventories decline, customer restocking broadens and prices recover. The previous report judged demand weak.

Evasive: “Some indicators have improved, but persistence remains uncertain, so we maintain our previous view.”

Appropriate: “The sector has entered a demand-led recovery, warranting an upgraded outlook for the next two quarters. Improving orders, volumes, inventory drawdowns and prices show that the recovery is broadening; stronger volumes and prices will continue to support earnings.”

After checking policy front-loading, seasonality and base effects, change the view explicitly if positive evidence still dominates. This skill does not imply a default cautious or bearish stance.

## 8. Move from one channel to net effects and market implications

Given: deposit yields fall, but some households also face lower loan-interest payments. Comparing exposures, repricing schedules and consumption propensities shows that lost deposit income has a larger spending effect than debt-service relief. Markets have fully anticipated this easing, and delivery matches those expectations.

Overstated: “Deposit-rate cuts reduce interest income, so consumption must fall; easing means stocks must rise.”

Appropriate: “Deposit and loan repricing create a net drag on consumption. Debt-service relief improves borrowers' cash flows, but the negative effect of lost deposit income dominates after accounting for exposures, repricing and spending propensities. This easing was fully anticipated, leaving limited incremental valuation support from policy delivery itself; subsequent market performance will depend more on earnings improvement.”

These conclusions depend on the completed net-effect and market-expectations checks in the scenario; do not apply them mechanically. Complete the evidence comparison and state which force dominates in the actual report. If the aggregate net effect remains unidentified, conclude clearly on established group-level effects; do not report an unknown aggregate effect as zero. Economic outcomes and market prices each require their own evidence.
